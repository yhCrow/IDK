# 1.1 Kinematics <span class="zh">运动学</span>

Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it.

## Key quantities

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit | Scalar / vector |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{Distance|路程}} | $d$ | m | Scalar |
| {{Displacement|位移}} | $s$ | m | Vector |
| {{Speed|速率}} | — | m s⁻¹ | Scalar |
| {{Velocity|速度}} | $u, v$ | m s⁻¹ | Vector |
| {{Acceleration|加速度}} | $a$ | m s⁻² | Vector |

$$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} \qquad a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

## Equations of uniformly accelerated motion <span class="zh">匀变速直线运动公式</span>

These only work when **acceleration is constant**. $u$ = initial velocity, $v$ = final velocity.

$$v = u + at$$
$$s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$$
$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$
$$s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)\,t$$

**How to choose:** write down the five symbols $s, u, v, a, t$, fill in the three you know, and pick the equation that does not contain the one you don't need.

## Motion graphs

| Graph | Gradient means | Area under means |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement–time ($s$–$t$) | Velocity | — |
| Velocity–time ($v$–$t$) | Acceleration | Displacement |
| Acceleration–time ($a$–$t$) | — | Change in velocity |

## Free fall <span class="zh">自由落体</span>

Without air resistance, all objects fall with the same {{acceleration of free fall|重力加速度}}:

$$g \approx 9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2} \quad (\text{often taken as } 10\ \text{m s}^{-2})$$

## Projectile motion <span class="zh">抛体运动</span>

Treat horizontal and vertical motion **separately** — they share only the time $t$.

- Horizontal: constant velocity, $x = u_x t$
- Vertical: constant acceleration $g$ downwards, $y = u_y t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$

## Worked example

> A car moving at $20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ brakes with a deceleration of $4\ \text{m s}^{-2}$. Find the stopping time and stopping distance.

$u = 20$, $v = 0$, $a = -4$.

Time: $v = u + at \Rightarrow 0 = 20 - 4t \Rightarrow t = 5\ \text{s}$

Distance: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 0 = 400 - 8s \Rightarrow s = 50\ \text{m}$

> **Exam tip:** Choose a positive direction at the start and keep to it. A deceleration is just a negative $a$.
