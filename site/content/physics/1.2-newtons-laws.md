# 1.2 Newton's Laws & Momentum <span class="zh">牛顿运动定律与动量</span>

## Newton's three laws

| Law | Statement |
|---|---|
| **First law** ({{inertia|惯性}}) | An object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a {{resultant force|合力}} acts on it. |
| **Second law** | The resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum: $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$. For constant mass, $F = ma$. |
| **Third law** | If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A. |

**Newton's third law pairs** ({{action and reaction|作用力与反作用力}}) are:

- equal in size and opposite in direction,
- the **same type** of force (both gravitational, both contact…),
- acting on **different** bodies — so they never cancel out.

> **Common trap:** A book's weight and the table's normal force on the book are **not** a third-law pair — they act on the same body. The pair of the book's weight is the book's gravitational pull on the **Earth**.

## Forces you should know

| Force | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| {{Weight|重力}} | $W = mg$ | Acts through the centre of gravity |
| {{Friction|摩擦力}} | $f \le \mu N$ (static), $f = \mu N$ (kinetic) | Opposes relative motion |
| {{Normal force|法向力}} | — | Perpendicular to the surface |
| On a slope (angle $\theta$) | Along slope $mg\sin\theta$, into slope $mg\cos\theta$ | Resolve weight into components |

## Momentum <span class="zh">动量</span>

$$p = mv \qquad (\text{unit: kg m s}^{-1}, \text{ a vector})$$

**Principle of conservation of momentum:** in a closed system (no external force), the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after.

$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

| Collision | Momentum conserved? | Kinetic energy conserved? |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic | ✓ | ✓ |
| Inelastic | ✓ | ✗ |

## Impulse <span class="zh">冲量</span>

$$\text{Impulse} = F\Delta t = \Delta p \qquad (\text{unit: N s})$$

Impulse is also the **area under a force–time graph**. Crumple zones and airbags increase $\Delta t$, which reduces the force for the same $\Delta p$.

## Worked example

> A 2 kg block on a rough floor is pulled by a horizontal force of 10 N. Friction is 4 N. Find the acceleration.

Resultant force $= 10 - 4 = 6\ \text{N}$, so $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3\ \text{m s}^{-2}$.

> **Exam tip:** Draw a free-body diagram first. $F = ma$ uses the **resultant** force, not just the applied force.
