# 2.1 Current Electricity <span class="zh">电流与电路</span>

## Basic quantities

| Quantity | Definition | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| {{Current|电流}} | Rate of flow of charge | $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$ | ampere (A) |
| {{Potential difference|电势差}} | Energy transferred per unit charge | $V = \dfrac{W}{Q}$ | volt (V) |
| {{Resistance|电阻}} | Ratio of p.d. to current | $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$ | ohm (Ω) |
| {{Power|功率}} | Rate of energy transfer | $P = IV$ | watt (W) |

## Ohm's law <span class="zh">欧姆定律</span>

The current through a metallic conductor is **directly proportional** to the potential difference across it, **provided the temperature stays constant**.

- An ohmic conductor gives a straight line through the origin on an $I$–$V$ graph.
- A filament lamp's graph curves: as it heats up, its resistance increases.

## Resistivity <span class="zh">电阻率</span>

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Longer wire → more resistance. Thicker wire (larger $A$) → less resistance.

## Series and parallel circuits

| | {{Series|串联}} | {{Parallel|并联}} |
|---|---|---|
| Current | Same everywhere | Splits: $I = I_1 + I_2$ |
| Voltage | Shared: $V = V_1 + V_2$ | Same across each branch |
| Resistance | $R = R_1 + R_2 + \dots$ | $\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2} + \dots$ |

Quick fact: two equal resistors $R$ in parallel give $R/2$.

## Electrical power and energy

$$P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R} \qquad E = Pt$$

Electricity bills use the **kilowatt-hour**: $1\ \text{kWh} = 1000\ \text{W} \times 3600\ \text{s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\ \text{J}$.

## E.m.f. and internal resistance <span class="zh">电动势与内电阻</span>

$$E = I(R + r) = V + Ir$$

$E$ is the {{electromotive force|电动势}}, $r$ is the {{internal resistance|内电阻}}, and $V = IR$ is the terminal potential difference. $Ir$ is the "lost volts" inside the cell.

## Worked example

> A cell has e.m.f. 6 V and internal resistance 1 Ω. It is connected to a 5 Ω resistor. Find the current and the terminal p.d.

$I = \dfrac{E}{R + r} = \dfrac{6}{5 + 1} = 1\ \text{A}$, so $V = IR = 1 \times 5 = 5\ \text{V}$ (1 V is lost inside the cell).

> **Exam tip:** An ammeter goes in **series** (ideally zero resistance). A voltmeter goes in **parallel** (ideally infinite resistance).
